# Domain playbook

## Select the method

1. For a conjecture-led project, freeze the statement, allowed assumptions and known special cases. Build a dependency graph with proof obligations; an edge means one result genuinely needs another, not simply that the topics are related.
2. For an experiment-led project, predefine discriminating outcomes and record failed approaches, versions, seeds and arithmetic. A numerical observation becomes a conjecture until supported analytically or by certified coverage.
3. For a deadline-led project, define bounded deliverables such as a checked special case, literature comparison or reproducible implementation. Use milestones to decide continuation or pivot; do not assign a guaranteed completion date to an unproved theorem.

## Worked check

Suppose a project seeks a sharp inequality and already proves a bound of 3 while a witness gives ratio 2. The unresolved interval is [2,3]. A useful next milestone is either prove an upper bound 2 or produce a witness exceeding 2. Each outcome narrows the mathematical uncertainty; polishing the bound 3 repeatedly does not.

## Invalid inference and witness

A completed list of computations does not mean the main dependency is resolved. The first hundred values of n²+n+41 are not a proof of primality for all n; n=41 gives 41²+41+41=41·43. Track “tested values” separately from “proved universal lemma.”

## Completion and handoff

Deliver a current state ledger, dependency graph, bounded next actions, evidence status and pivot triggers. When source access or a proof is missing, mark that dependency blocked rather than scheduling downstream claims as complete. Hand literature work exact comparator statements and proof work the smallest unresolved lemma, retaining unsuccessful approaches to prevent repeated effort.
