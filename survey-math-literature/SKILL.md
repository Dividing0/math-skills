# Survey Math Literature

## Workflow

1. Define the research question, vocabulary, equivalent formulations, subject area and search boundaries. Search both foundational work and recent extensions.
2. Retrieve primary papers and authoritative mathematical texts; inspect theorem statements and proofs rather than relying on abstracts or citation counts.
3. Record title, authors, date, version, stable identifier, theorem or page location, hypotheses and conclusion. Distinguish preprints, published versions, corrections and retractions when verifiable.
4. Build a comparison table of assumptions, strength of conclusions, methods and limitations. Check whether similarly named objects use different conventions.
5. Trace decisive citations to their original source. Label unavailable full texts and claims supported only by secondary summaries.
6. Report search scope and date, established results, contradictions and open leads. Do not claim global novelty or that no result exists merely because searches found none.

## Evidence and output discipline

Maintain a ledger separating given facts, verified external results, proved claims, conjectures and computational observations. State assumptions and unresolved obligations explicitly. Cite external mathematical results with a verifiable source and precise locator when available; do not invent references. Read supplied papers before relying on their contents. Use tools only when they improve verification and state which checks actually ran. Respect the user’s language.

Read [acceptance-cases.md](references/acceptance-cases.md) to check the intended boundary behavior before completing a task. Hand off downstream work with the exact statement, assumptions, evidence and remaining obligations; load other skills only when needed.
