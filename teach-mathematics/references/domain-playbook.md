# Teaching with diagnostic evidence

## Choose a branch
- Concept introduction: identify prerequisite vocabulary and the operation the learner should perform afterward. Start with one transparent example, extract the definition, then contrast a nearby nonexample. State conventions before relying on them.
- Proof instruction: break a proof into the claim, hypotheses and decisive inference. Ask the learner to supply a missing step rather than only copy a finished argument; distinguish motivation from a proof covering all quantified cases.
- Remediation or transfer: use a learner's actual answer to locate a misconception, then choose a task whose result distinguishes the competing ideas. If no response exists, offer a diagnostic question and avoid claiming the misconception or mastery was observed.

## Worked explanation
To teach the difference between a square and an absolute value, assume real x and define sqrt as the nonnegative root. For x=3, sqrt(x²)=3; for x=-3, sqrt(x²)=3, so the general answer is |x|. Proof: |x|>=0 and |x|²=x², hence |x| is the nonnegative root. Next ask the learner to simplify sqrt((t-2)²) for t<2. This task checks transfer to a shifted expression, rather than repeating the original numbers. Give a hint about the sign before a solution if the learner requested practice.

## Tempting inference and counterexample
A correct numerical answer can mask an invalid rule. A learner says sqrt(a+b)=sqrt(a)+sqrt(b); at a=0,b=4 the answer 2 happens to be correct. At a=b=1 it predicts 2 instead of sqrt(2), exposing the false distributive rule. Diagnose from the explanation and a discriminating example, not from one successful output. Conversely one arithmetic slip does not demonstrate failure to understand the underlying concept.

## Stop or hand off
If prerequisites are unknown, state the assumed level and provide a brief check instead of loading an advanced proof. For assessment, hand off unseen transfer tasks and a rubric to exercise design; keep worked solutions unavailable until appropriate. Preserve the learner's language, requested hint policy and mathematical domain. Report that material was explained or exercises supplied; report learning only after observing relevant learner responses. A short lesson should contain a target competency and a meaningful check, not an unrelated research dossier.
